In Short
  • Non-communicable diseases cause 56.7% of deaths; heart disease alone causes 31%[1].
  • 10.1 crore Indians have diabetes and 31.5 crore have high blood pressure[2].
  • Nearly 15.7 lakh new cancer cases were estimated for 2025[3]; road crashes kill about 20 people an hour[4].
  • Patients pay about half of current health spending out of their own pockets, and that share rose in 2022–23 as government spending fell back after Covid[5].

The digital rails are large: 127.5 crore health records are linked to ABHA IDs, and 6.09 lakh facilities and 13.98 lakh professionals are verified[8]. What is still thin is the record written at the point of care, in a two-minute consultation[9].

Life and Death

72.2 yearsLife expectancy at birth, 2024World Bank[10]
24Infant deaths per 1,000 live births in 2024, down from 25 in 2023 and 40 in 2013SRS 2024[11][12]
28Deaths before age five per 1,000 live births in 2024SRS 2024[11]

Progress is real but uneven. In 2024 infant mortality was 8 per 1,000 in Kerala, 35 in Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh, and 36 in Chhattisgarh. About 1 rural baby in 37 dies before its first birthday, against about 1 in 59 in towns and cities. Newborn deaths, in the first four weeks, are 18 per 1,000 and make up nearly three-quarters of infant deaths[13].

How Many Indians Die, and of What

about 90 lakhDeaths in India in 2024: 89.4 lakh were registered, and about 99 in 100 deaths are now registeredCRS 2024[14]; our estimate
about 25,000Deaths every day, about one every 3 to 4 secondsour estimate from the yearly total
23%Share of registered deaths in 2024 whose cause was certified by a doctor, up from 22% in 2023MCCD 2024[15]

The total is close to the government's own sample survey, which counts 6.4 deaths a year for every 1,000 people in 2024[11]. For more than three deaths in four, no doctor certifies the cause. So, to see what Indians die of, the Sample Registration System sends trained surveyors to households in a fixed sample of villages and towns to ask about each death, and doctors assign the cause[1]. Air pollution is a risk, not a separate cause. About 21 lakh deaths in India in 2021 were linked to polluted air, including 1.69 lakh children under five[27]. Most are counted above as heart, lung or infection deaths.

What the gaps say. Snakebite deaths are counted as about 10,000 by the police and about 58,000 by careful household study. Dengue deaths are 297 in one government count and 2,340 in another. When most deaths happen without a doctor's certificate, and the medical history of the person is not written down anywhere, the country cannot see what is killing its people. A signed record of every visit is part of the fix.

In men, lip and oral cavity cancer (about 15.6% of new cases) and lung cancer (about 8.5%) lead[30]. Survival depends heavily on early detection, which depends on someone noticing a pattern across visits. More on how other countries achieve higher survival is on the comparison page.

Road Accidents and Emergencies

4,87,707Road accidents in 2024, up 1.5% on 2023MoRTH[4][32]
1,77,175People killed, up 2.5%; about 20 every hourMoRTH[4][32]
4,71,441People injuredMoRTH[4]

Since May 2025, road accident victims anywhere in India can get cashless treatment of up to ₹1.5 lakh for seven days at designated hospitals[33]. Paying for the first week solves one problem; knowing the victim's blood group, allergies and medicines is another. That is what an emergency card is for.

What Illness Costs Families

5.5 croreIndians pushed into poverty by health spending in a single year (2011–12 survey data); 3.8 crore by medicines alonePHFI study in The BMJ, 2018[34]
80%+Indians with no health expenditure coverage in 2017–18NSS 75th round[35]
80%Rural families who paid for a hospital stay from savings; 13% borrowedNSS 75th round[35]

Medicines are the largest single cause of impoverishment[34]. A record that prevents a duplicate prescription or a repeated test saves money for the family that pays. These studies are old: insurance has expanded since 2017–18, so treat them as a baseline, not today's numbers. The newest national accounts show families paying 49.9% of current health spending in 2022–23, up from 45.1% a year earlier[5].

Who Pays, and Where People Go First

45.5 croreAyushman Bharat (PM-JAY) cards issued by 12 August 2026, covering up to ₹5 lakh of hospital care per family a yearPIB, August 2026[36]
12.69 croreHospital admissions covered under PM-JAY by 30 June 2026, worth ₹1.92 lakh crore, at 38,466 listed public and private hospitalsPIB, August 2026[36]
1.78 lakhAyushman Arogya Mandirs, the government's local primary health centres, open as of July 2025Lok Sabha[37]
818Medical colleges, up from 387 in 2014; MBBS seats rose from 51,348 to 1,28,875Rajya Sabha, December 2025[38]

PM-JAY pays for hospital stays, not for outpatient visits or most medicines, which is where Indian families spend most of their own money[34]. In 2024 the scheme was opened to everyone aged 70 and over; 1.20 crore older people had enrolled by August 2026[36], out of about 6 crore in that age group[39]. Kerala and Karnataka are exceptions (see the schemes page). The first point of contact for most illnesses remains a small private clinic or a local health centre, the setting 1Heath is built for.

How each state adds to PM-JAY, and how to use a scheme step by step, is on the government health schemes page. Whether India has enough doctors, nurses and beds, state by state and against the world, is on the workforce and beds page.

Children's Nutrition

29.3%Children under five who are stunted, too short for their age from long-term undernutrition; down from 35.5% in 2019–21NFHS-6, 2023–24[40]
31.8%Children under five who are underweight, barely down from 32.1%; 19% are wasted, too thin for their heightNFHS-6, 2023–24[40]

In the same survey, adults moved the other way: 30.7% of women and 27.3% of men aged 15 to 49 are now overweight or obese, up from 24% and 22.9%[40]. NFHS-6 has not yet released its anaemia figures[41]. India carries both problems at once. Why, and what has to change, is on the page why Indians fall ill. A child's weight and growth, recorded at every visit, are what a paediatrician needs to dose medicines safely and to notice faltering growth early.

Mothers and Inherited Disease

87Maternal deaths per lakh live births in 2022–24, down from 130 in 2014–16SRS special bulletin[23][42]
10,000–15,000Children born with thalassaemia major each year; 1 to 1.5 lakh living with itUnion Health Minister, 2020[43]

A woman arriving at night in labour may not be able to give her blood group, a previous caesarean or her blood pressure history. Thalassaemia major is preventable when carriers know their status before having children, which requires family records that link, with consent.

Why are these diseases so common in India, and what do countries with lower rates do differently? See India's biggest diseases.

What This Means for Medical Records

  • Long-term disease dominates. Heart disease, diabetes and high blood pressure need years of medicines from several doctors. A record that follows the patient prevents duplicates and omissions.
  • Patients pay. With about half of current health spending out of pocket[5], every repeated test and every avoidable admission is paid by a family.
  • Emergencies need four facts fast. Blood group, allergies, current medicines, long-term conditions.
  • Families share risk. Inherited conditions need linked family records, used with consent and counselling.
