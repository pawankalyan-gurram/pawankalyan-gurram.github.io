High-Level Design

One Consultation, End to End

  1. Swipe to start. Consent is already recorded at the desk. Listening begins.
  2. Capture and chunk. The phone records 16 kHz mono audio; a voice-activity detector cuts it at natural pauses into chunks of up to about 10 seconds.
  3. Transcribe. Each chunk goes through the gateway to speech recognition in translate mode, which returns English in natural word order.
  4. Gate. If the English contains a catalogue name, a molecule, a number or a dosing word, the same audio is sent once more in code-mixed mode to keep the original words and numbers. The last chunk after Stop is sent to both at once.
  5. Live strips. As chunks return, the screen fills two strips: "Already taking" (what the patient says they take) and "Today's orders".
  6. Draft. On Stop, both transcripts go to the drafting model, which returns a structured note: complaints, history, examination, diagnosis, current medicines with an action, and prescription lines with every field.
  7. Verify on the phone. The rules engine checks every line and assigns a state: verified, confirm, or cannot sign.
  8. History paragraph. Written only from verified facts.
  9. Fallback. If the model fails or takes longer than 10 seconds, a rules-only note is shown and every medicine needs confirmation.
  10. Review and sign. The doctor resolves flagged lines and slides to sign.
  11. Store and print. The signed record, each line's spoken words, its interpretation, every reason given and the catalogue source are stored; the prescription PDF is generated.

Cost and Connectivity

QuestionAnswer
Speech cost per consultationSarvam charges ₹0.49 a minute before GST[1]. That is about ₹0.50 a minute for translation alone, and about ₹0.81 a minute with the second original-language pass, which ran on about 62% of our synthetic audio[2]. A two- to five-minute consultation costs roughly ₹1 to ₹4 in speech fees.
Drafting cost per consultationNot yet measured; it will be measured on cached transcripts before any pricing decision. Our rough estimate: if a note reads about 10,000 tokens (pieces of words) and writes about 2,000 (our assumption), it costs about ₹0.10 plus ₹0.05, so about 15 paise, at Sarvam's introductory price of ₹10 and ₹25 per 10 lakh tokens, which runs to the end of 2026[1].
What if the internet is slow or down?The demo needs internet to transcribe. If the drafting model fails or takes longer than 10 seconds, the doctor gets a rules-only note in which every medicine must be confirmed. There is no offline mode yet; whether one is needed is a question for the pilot.
How many doctors at once?Sarvam's pay-as-you-go plan allows 60 speech requests a minute[1]. With chunks of about 10 seconds and a second pass on about 62% of them, one doctor sends about 10 requests a minute (our estimate), so about six doctors can speak at the same moment before a higher plan is needed.
Can the model run on India's own infrastructure?Yes in principle: the drafting model's weights are open under Apache 2.0[3][4], so it can be self-hosted if a hospital or the law requires it, though a model this size needs data-centre graphics processors.

How the AI Cost per Visit Can Fall Below ₹1

Almost all of the AI cost is speech recognition, and it is billed by the second of speech sent. The app already sends only speech, not silence. So the cost per visit depends on how long people talk. Our test consultations are 3.5 to 5 minutes of continuous talk and cost about ₹1.90 to ₹4.60 each in speech fees, which is where the ₹3.09 figure comes from[5]. Studies put a typical Indian primary-care visit at about two minutes[6], so a real visit may cost less; the pilot will measure it.

Our estimates. Each row adds about ₹0.15 for drafting the note (see above), from estimated token counts, not a measured average; GST, retries and running costs are not included. Dollar prices use about ₹90 to the dollar, our assumption. The own-card row assumes the card is busy at least a fifth of the time and transcribes far faster than real time; neither is measured.

The order to try: first measure real visit length in the pilot; then test one pass against two on the same recordings; then ask Sarvam for volume pricing; then test an open model and a cheaper service on the same recordings, keeping the one that loses no allergy, dose or medicine name. Quality decides, not price: a cheaper model that drops an allergy is not cheaper. In the longer run we plan our own medical speech model within one to two years (the plan).

How It Is Built and Tested

  • Rulings live in the repository. Every product decision is written down, and a build checklist fails the build if a rule is broken.
  • About 1,788 automated tests across the apps and the engine, plus image tests that guard approved screens at normal size, 200% text, tablet and stress layouts.
  • A frozen clinical test set of 131 cases, not yet run; any change to an existing test needs the founder's written acceptance. Results so far come from 14 unseen simulated consultations in Hindi and Telugu and 85 spoken prescription lines (see models and data).
  • A benchmark harness that replays cached transcripts, so most accuracy changes are measured without new speech costs.
  • Done means verified on a real Android phone through the real app with a real gesture.

The Low-Level Design

The detailed engineering design (the data model, how spoken medicine names are matched, every verification rule, encryption and keys, and how the system scales) is kept in a private blueprint, because it describes security in detail. It is shared with the team, technical advisers and investors on request; the contact details are on the home page.
