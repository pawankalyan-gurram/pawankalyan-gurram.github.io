Roadmap: From Scribe to Health Research Company

The four stages, the prevention features to come, and how 1Heath becomes a research and development company without ever selling patient data.

By Pawan KalyanUpdated 25 November 2026Page 20 of 206-minute read20 sources

In Short
  • Four stages: a safe scribe for one clinic; one record across doctor, desk, pharmacy and patient; checks that prevent harm; and, with consent and ethics approval, research that makes Indian medicine safer.
  • Each stage starts only when the one before is measured on real patients, not when it is built.
  • 1Heath is the first product of a healthcare research and development company. The next product turns CT and MRI scans into accurate, interactive 3D models; after that, other real-world health problems, one at a time.
  • Patients get their record free; later they may pay for medicine delivery and a care membership that looks after food, activity and long-term illness between visits.
  • Patient data is never sold. Research uses only consented, de-identified data, under ethics review, with results published.

Stage 4: A Health Research Company

About half of Indians use AYUSH or home remedies[6], yet no record anywhere holds what one patient takes from every system at once, so questions about combined use and harm are hard to study. A signed, structured, consented record across every system of medicine makes them answerable.

Research programmeQuestion it answersPartner type
Medicine safety signalsWhich combinations, including Ayurvedic and modern medicines, are followed by harm?National pharmacovigilance programmes, including the AYUSH programme[7]
Indian clinical languageHow do Indian doctors and patients describe illness and medicines across languages?Universities, speech and language researchers
Antibiotic useWhere and why are broad-spectrum antibiotics prescribed, and does prompting change it?Public-health researchers; national estimates rest mainly on sales data[4], and data on what clinics actually prescribe is thin (our reading)
Real-world outcomesHow do Indian patients respond to common treatments outside trials?Medical colleges, government research programmes such as PRIP[8]
Trial recruitmentCan patients who opt in be told about trials they qualify for?Hospitals and sponsors, with ethics approval

Research Rules

  • 1

    Separate, explicit consent for research, never bundled with care. Refusing changes nothing about the patient's care.

  • 2

    De-identified data in a controlled environment; no raw records leave it.

  • 3

    Independent ethics review of every study, and a data-access committee that includes doctors and patients.

  • 4

    Results published, including negative ones.

  • 5

    Never sold. Partners fund studies; they never buy data.

Later: Services Patients Pay For

Only after the clinic product is proven, and never by charging for safety or for the record itself:

  • Medicines delivered home: refills of a signed prescription, from the pharmacy the patient chooses each time (the clinic's counter, a nearby chemist or a licensed online pharmacy, shown side by side with none chosen in advance). Online-pharmacy rules are still a 2018 draft[9], so this waits for a lawyer's opinion.
  • A care membership: follow-up for diabetes, blood pressure and other long-term illness, led by the patient's own doctor, with nurses and dietitians, reminders, refills, and food and activity plans. Software alone has not improved control in India[10]; steady medicines with regular follow-up have[11]. It needs a research team and evidence that it works for Indian patients.

How each of these fits with doctors, clinics and hospitals: the patient's point of view.

The Company Behind 1Heath

1Heath is the first product of a healthcare research and development company that takes real problems in Indian healthcare, researches them in depth and builds tools that doctors and patients can trust. Each product follows the same rules: research before building, measured on real patients before it is claimed, and never selling patient data.

ProductThe problemStatus
1HeathDoctors treat patients without their history, in about two minutes a visitIn development; a demo app exists; not yet used with real patients
Scans to 3D modelsCT and MRI scans are read as hundreds of flat slices. Accurate, interactive 3D models of the patient's own anatomy help surgeons plan and patients understand. In orthopaedics, a review of 65 studies found shorter operations, less blood loss and less X-ray screening with 3D models, though results varied widely[12]; in jaw and face surgery, most studies lacked a comparison group[13]. The likely first use is one narrow job done well, such as CT models of complex pelvic fractures, reviewed by a surgeon before useResearch. Software that informs surgical planning is regulated as a medical device in India, so its class must be confirmed with CDSCO first[14]
Next problemsChosen from what doctors, hospitals and patients tell us, one at a timeNot started

Our Own Medical Speech Model, Within One to Two Years

Speech recognition is nearly all of the AI cost of a visit (worked out here). Today 1Heath uses Sarvam's Saaras, a general-purpose speech model for Indian languages: good on everyday Hindi, Telugu and mixed speech, but not trained for medicine names, doses and clinical shorthand. We will use it, or another service, for the first one to two years, and build our own speech model made for Indian clinics in that time.

QuestionOur plan
Why our own modelLower cost (about ₹0.15 to ₹0.35 a visit instead of about ₹1 to ₹1.75, our estimate), better accuracy on medicine names and doses, and data that stays in India. In one study, training a general model further on clinical speech doubled how often medicine names came out right, from about 34% to 66%[15].
Build from scratch?No. Start from an open model with a free licence, such as AI4Bharat's IndicConformer (MIT licence, 22 Indian languages)[16], and train it further on clinical speech. That is months of work for a small team, not years (our estimate).
What it needsConsented recordings of real consultations with ethics approval, each typed out and checked by a doctor or pharmacist; a held-back test set never used for training; a speech engineer; and graphics cards rented by the hour[17].
When we switchOnly when our model beats the paid service on the same recordings for allergies, doses, negatives and medicine names, not just on overall word errors. Until then the paid service stays.
Who else is doing itEka Care has built Parrotlet, a clinical speech model for mixed Hindi and English[18], and is working with NVIDIA on one that runs offline on the phone[19]. So it can be done, and we are not first.
Decided: keep consented audioThe demo keeps no clinical audio. From the pilot on, recordings may be kept to train the model, but only with each patient's written consent, an ethics committee's approval, registration on the Clinical Trials Registry of India and storage in India (founder decision, 25 November 2026). No recording is kept until all four are in place, and a patient who says no is treated the same.

The same applies later to the drafting model: its weights are open[20], so it too can be hosted in India and trained further once enough consented, de-identified consultations exist.
