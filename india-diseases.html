India's Biggest Diseases: Why They Are High, and What Works

Diabetes, high blood pressure and heart disease, tuberculosis, tobacco and infection-linked cancers, and anaemia: the reasons, the countries that do better, and the programmes that work.

In Short
  • Five conditions dominate: diabetes, high blood pressure and heart disease, TB, tobacco- and infection-linked cancers, and anaemia.
  • India's age-adjusted diabetes rate (10.5%) is below the world average and the US[1]; the problem is risk at lower body weight[2] and late diagnosis[3].
  • India has a quarter of the world's TB[4]; TB deaths fell to about 2.63 lakh in 2025[5].
  • Air pollution (21 lakh deaths in 2021[6]) and untreated mental illness (70–92% treatment gap[7]) add two more large burdens.
  • Countries that do better find people early, treat simply and follow up.

Diabetes

8.98 croreAdults aged 20 to 79 with diabetes in 2024, about 1 in 7 of the world's totalIDF Diabetes Atlas 2025[3]
43%Of them undiagnosed, about 3.86 crore peopleIDF Diabetes Atlas 2025[3]
15.67 croreProjected for 2050, a 75% increaseIDF Diabetes Atlas 2025[3]

The ICMR-INDIAB study, using its own survey and definitions, estimated 10.1 crore people with diabetes and another 13.6 crore with prediabetes[9]. The two estimates differ in method and age range; both say the same thing about scale.

Is India's Diabetes Rate Unusually High?

Not compared with other large countries. Once ages are adjusted so countries can be compared fairly, India's rate is below the world average and below the United States'. Several European countries are much lower.

Why Pakistan Is Highest

Pakistan has the world's highest adult diabetes rate. Its doctors and policy researchers point to sedentary lives (41.4% of people, and 53.7% of women, miss WHO activity guidelines), carbohydrate- and sugar-heavy diets, no control of free sugars or industrial trans fats, fast urbanisation, and genetic risk deepened by marriage between close relatives; more than a third of cases are undiagnosed[17]. Several of these apply to parts of India too.

Why India's Problem Is Still Serious

  • Body type. Standard obesity cut-offs miss South Asians. In England, the diabetes risk a white adult has at a BMI of 30 is reached by South Asian adults at 23.9[2]. Many Indians who look slim are at risk.
  • Diet. Indians get 62% of their calories from carbohydrates and about 12% from protein. Refined cereals were linked to 13% higher odds of diabetes, and added sugar to 14% higher odds; added sugar exceeded the national limit of 5% of calories in 21 states and union territories[18]. It is not only sugar: white rice and refined wheat matter as much.
  • Late diagnosis. Nearly half of people with diabetes have not been diagnosed[3].

High Blood Pressure and Heart Disease

Cardiovascular disease causes 31% of all deaths in India, more than any other cause[10]. High blood pressure is its biggest driver, and 31.5 crore Indians have it[9]. The problem is rarely the medicine; cheap, effective tablets exist. The problem is the chain from diagnosis to lasting control.

What the Best Countries and India's Best Districts Do

  • Find people. Worldwide in 2019, 41% of women and 51% of men with high blood pressure had not been diagnosed[11].
  • Treat by a simple protocol. India's Hypertension Control Initiative uses a fixed drug-and-dose protocol, free medicines and care close to home. In its districts, control rose from 37% to 48%, and the share of patients paying for medicines fell from 47% to 9%[21].
  • Keep them on treatment. Under the 75 by 25 initiative, 4.20 crore people with high blood pressure were on standard treatment[20].

Why TB Stays High in India

WHO names five drivers of TB: undernutrition, HIV, diabetes, smoking and alcohol use disorders[4]. India has all five at scale, along with crowded housing that spreads infection. In the United States, 77% of cases are in people born abroad, and the rate among US-born people is 0.8 per lakh[29]: where nutrition and housing are good and cases are found fast, TB nearly disappears.

Cancers Driven by Tobacco and Infection

India has far fewer new cancers per person than Denmark or Australia (about 98 against 350 per lakh, age-adjusted)[32], yet about 3 in 5 Indians diagnosed with cancer die of it[33], and India's cancer death rate rose about 21% between 1990 and 2023 while the world's fell[34]. The difference is late diagnosis: only 1.9% of Indian women had ever been screened for cervical cancer in 2019–21[35]; the 2023–24 survey did not publish screening figures[36]. Tobacco and Oral Cancer

Cervical Cancer: A Cancer That Can Be Eliminated

Cervical cancer is caused by HPV infection, so it can be prevented by vaccination and caught early by screening. Australia began HPV vaccination in 2007, moved to HPV-based screening, recorded no cervical cancer in women under 25 in 2021, and is on track to eliminate the disease as a public health problem by 2035. Even there, vaccination coverage has slipped to just under 80% and screening participation is falling[15].

India began a nationwide single-dose HPV vaccination drive for 14-year-old girls in 2026, aiming to reach the 1.15 crore girls who turn 14 each year. Ministry officials cite nearly 80,000 new cases and more than 42,000 deaths a year[39]; GLOBOCAN 2024 estimates 79,360 new cases and 36,319 deaths[38].

Source: National Family Health Survey[16]. Anaemia rose in every group between the two surveys. The next survey, NFHS-6 (2023–24, fact sheets released May 2026), dropped the blood test, so it gives no anaemia figures and NFHS-5 remains the latest national measure[36].

India's Anaemia Mukt Bharat programme, launched in 2018, combines six interventions for six age groups: iron and folic acid supplements, deworming, year-round behaviour change, digital testing and treatment, fortified foods in government programmes, and treatment of non-nutritional causes where they are common[40]. Anaemia in pregnancy raises the risk of bleeding and of low birth weight, which is why a mother's haemoglobin belongs in her record.

Two More Large Burdens: Air Pollution and Mental Health

Air Pollution

21 lakhDeaths in India linked to air pollution in 2021State of Global Air 2024[6]
1,69,400Children under five in India whose deaths were linked to air pollution in 2021, the highest of any countryState of Global Air 2024[6]

Air pollution is not a disease but a risk factor behind heart disease, stroke, lung disease and childhood pneumonia. Fine particles from burning fossil fuels and biomass cause most of the harm[6]. For a doctor, the useful record is the patient's exposure (cooking fuel, occupation) alongside the diagnosis.

Mental Health

10.6%Adults in India with a mental disorderNational Mental Health Survey 2015–16[7]
70–92%People with a mental disorder who receive no treatment, depending on the disorderNational Mental Health Survey[7]

Mental illness can first appear in a general clinic as headache, sleeplessness or body pain. A record that shows the pattern across visits helps the doctor recognise it.

The Common Thread

India already runs serious programmes for every one of these conditions. Where they work, it is because each patient is found, treated and followed up. Follow-up is where a missing record does the most damage, and it is the step 1Heath is designed for: a record written at every visit, from the conversation, that the next doctor can see.
