1Heath: The Visit Writes the History

A medical scribe and shared record for India's small clinics. The doctor talks; 1Heath writes a checked note and prescription in the doctor's language; the doctor slides to sign; the record follows the patient.

The problem
Studies put a typical Indian primary-care visit at about two minutes[1], the history lives on paper slips the patient may not bring, and about half of Indians used an AYUSH system in the past year[2], which rarely appears in any record. Our model, not a count, puts hospital deaths from medication errors caused by an incomplete history at about 6,300 a year, with a wide range (the method and its limits).
What it does
The doctor talks to the patient as usual, in English or any of 22 Indian languages, or a mix. 1Heath writes the note and prescription, checks every line against what was said and against the patient's record (allergies, current medicines from every system), and the doctor slides to sign. Nothing the patient says enters the record until the doctor confirms it. The signed prescription goes to the pharmacy app, the patient app or a WhatsApp link, and is not printed by default; the record follows the patient to the next clinic.
Why it is different
A language model drafts, but a rules engine decides: nothing reaches the note unless it was said, and anything uncertain is shown as a choice, never a guess. It is built for small Indian clinics first, with hospitals later: it works on the doctor's phone (online), in Indian languages, and records traditional medicines alongside modern ones.
Where it stands
The Doctor app works end to end on an Android phone. On 14 unseen simulated consultations in Hindi and Telugu, no invented allergy, refused medicine or wrong dose reached a note unflagged; the medicine-name matcher was also tested on 85 spoken prescription lines. The next test, not yet run, is a fixed set of 131 cases in eight languages. It has not yet been used with real patients. The Desk, Pharmacy and Patient apps are designed, not built. Next: the first clinic pilot with real voices, with patient consent.
How it earns
Free for patients, front desks and pharmacies. Doctors pay one membership under ₹500 a month that includes the scribe and cheaper supplies for their clinic pharmacy. Most revenue comes from that medicine supply, first through partner stockists and later as a licensed distributor for small and mid-size makers, and from flat monthly lab fees, never from influencing what is prescribed (the numbers, both cases).
Who is building it
Pawan Kalyan, a senior AI/ML and full-stack engineer, built the working Doctor app himself. Planned clinical advisors, by speciality: a physician with an MD in general medicine, who is to be the first to use it in his clinic; a paediatrician; and two younger doctors, one training in gynaecology. A small engineering team is planned once the company is funded.
What we need
General physicians, paediatricians and gynaecologists willing to try it in their clinic; pharmacists; and a first investor who sees healthcare as a service. Get in touch.

Terms Used on These Pages

ABHA
Ayushman Bharat Health Account: India's 14-digit health ID for each person.
ABDM
Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission: the government's system for health IDs, registries and consent-based record sharing.
AYUSH
Ayurveda, Yoga and Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha, Sowa-Rigpa and Homoeopathy.
OPD
Outpatient department: patients seen and sent home the same day.
Scribe
Software (or a person) that writes the clinical note from the conversation.
Verifier
1Heath's rules engine that checks each drafted line against the words spoken and the drug catalogue.
Anupana
In Ayurveda, the vehicle a medicine is taken with, such as warm water, honey or milk.
Potency
In homeopathy, the dilution of a remedy, such as 30C, 200 or 1M.
Look-alike names
Brand names that sound or look similar but are different drugs, a known cause of prescribing errors.
HLD and LLD
High-level design (the components) and low-level design (the data model and rules inside them).
FHIR
A global standard format for exchanging health records, used by ABDM.
DPDP Act
India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.
