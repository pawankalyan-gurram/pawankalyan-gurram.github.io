India's Healthcare Problems, and What 1Heath Does About Each

Fifteen ground-level problems in clinics, pharmacies, labs and homes, the evidence for each, what 1Heath does about it, how far that is built, and what software cannot fix.

In Short
  • One root problem: in India the patient's history does not travel. Each clinic, hospital, lab, chemist and system of medicine keeps its own slip of paper, and the doctor has about two minutes[1].
  • One answer: let the visit itself write the record. The doctor talks, 1Heath writes and checks, the doctor signs, and the record follows the patient to the pharmacy, the lab and the next doctor.
  • For everyone: small clinics first, then hospitals, corporate chains and government facilities; allopathy, Ayurveda and homeopathy on the same record.
  • Honest status: the Doctor app works as a demo. The Desk, Pharmacy and Patient apps are designed, not built. Nothing has been used with real patients yet.
  • What software cannot fix is said plainly at the end: too few doctors, nurses and beds, and too little public money.

Put together: a doctor with two minutes, a patient who cannot remember medicine names, half the story sitting with another system of medicine, and a family paying for every repeated test. The detail is in the problem and India's health in numbers.

What 1Heath Cannot Fix

  • Too few people. About 13 doctors, nurses and midwives are actually working for every 10,000 Indians, against a World Health Organization minimum of 44.5[16]. Software can save their time; it cannot create them.
  • Too few beds and specialists. Government hospitals have about 6 beds for every 10,000 people[17], and rural health centres are missing 4 in 5 of their specialists[18].
  • Too little public money. Government health spending was 1.43% of GDP in 2022–23[4].
  • Medicine quality at the factory, food, air and roads. See Indian healthcare for these.

What it can do is make each scarce minute, bed and rupee go further, by making sure nobody treats, tests or buys twice because a piece of paper was missing.
