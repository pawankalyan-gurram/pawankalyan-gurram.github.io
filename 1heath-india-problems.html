India's Healthcare Problems, and What 1Heath Does About Each

Twenty-three ground-level problems in clinics, pharmacies, labs and homes, the evidence for each, what 1Heath does about it, how far that is built, and what software cannot fix.

In Short
  • One root problem: in India the patient's history does not travel. Each clinic, hospital, lab, chemist and system of medicine keeps its own slip of paper, and the doctor has about two minutes[1].
  • One answer: let the visit itself write the record. The doctor talks, 1Heath writes and checks, the doctor signs, and the record is designed to follow the patient to the pharmacy, the lab and the next doctor. Today only the Doctor app exists, as a demo.
  • For everyone: small clinics first, then hospitals, corporate chains and government facilities; allopathy, Ayurveda and homeopathy on the same record.
  • Honest status: the Doctor app works as a demo. The Desk, Pharmacy and Patient apps are designed, not built. Nothing has been used with real patients yet.
  • Twenty-three problems are set out with evidence, from banned combinations and substandard batches to repeat tests, lost vaccination cards and rejected insurance claims, each with what 1Heath does and how far it is built.
  • What software cannot fix is said plainly at the end: too few doctors, nurses and beds, unqualified practitioners, and too little public money.

Put together: a doctor with two minutes, a patient who cannot remember medicine names, half the story sitting with another system of medicine, and a family paying for every repeated test. The detail is in the problem and India's health in numbers.

Twenty-Three Problems, and What 1Heath Does About Each

Status tells you how real each answer is today: Built means it works in the demo Doctor app; Designed means the screens and rules exist but the app is not built; Planned means later.

In the Consulting Room

ProblemEvidenceWhat 1Heath doesStatus
1. No time to take a historyVisits last about two minutes[1].The history is already on the screen; the visit adds to it by itself.Built in the demo, on test records; not used in a clinic

The doctor writes instead of listeningAbout 7 doctors (physicians) for every 10,000 people[5], so every minute counts.Uses a speech service that handles English and 22 Indian languages, mixed speech included, and drafts the note and prescription.Built; tested so far on 14 simulated consultations in Hindi and Telugu and 85 spoken prescription lines
3. Errors from missing historyUp to 27% of hospital prescribing errors[3].A rules engine checks every line: allergy, dose, duplicate, clash with regular medicines. The doctor signs every prescription.Built in the demo; checked on simulated cases only

Other systems of medicine are invisibleAbout half of Indians use them[2]; some metal-based Ayurvedic products carry real risk[7]."Also taking" records Ayurvedic, homeopathic and home remedies beside modern medicines. AYUSH doctors get their own case fields.Built for "also taking"; AYUSH doctor screens designed
5. No outpatient records keptThe national ethics rules require in-patient records only[8]. Some states go further: Telangana requires clinical establishments to keep records of patients treated for at least three years[9], but small clinics rarely keep them in a usable form.Every signed visit is a record, with no extra work.Built in the demo
6. Banned combinations still prescribedIn August 2024 the government banned 156 fixed-dose combinations, including antibiotics, painkillers and multivitamins, after an expert review found the harm outweighed the benefit[10], and 16 more in June 2026[11]. The list keeps changing, and old habits and old stock outlast a ban.A banned combination cannot be signed; the check says why and the doctor picks another medicine.Built in the demo on demo catalogue data; the full official list must be loaded and kept current before the pilot
7. Older patients on many medicinesIn six cities, 33.7% of 600 people aged 60 and above took many medicines at once, and 28.8% had a medicine that is risky at their age[12].One medicine list across every doctor and every system of medicine, with duplicate and clash checks on each new line.Duplicate and clash checks built; the list across doctors designed
8. TB cases from private clinics go unreportedTB must be notified since 2012, with penalties since 2018, and about half of people with TB symptoms first go to a private provider. Private doctors cite patient confidentiality, not knowing how, and getting no feedback[13].When the doctor diagnoses TB, the app prepares the Ni-kshay notification from the signed record for the doctor to send, and reminds until it is done. 1Heath never supplies TB medicines outside the national programme.Planned
9. Side effects never reportedDoctors, pharmacists and patients can all report a suspected reaction to the national programme, by form, app or helpline[14], but a busy clinic rarely does.A suspected reaction said in the visit is recorded with the medicine and its batch, and the app fills the national report for the doctor to send. A recurring problem with one batch shows up across clinics.Planned

22. The child's vaccination card is lostThe government's U-WIN portal has registered 11.05 crore children and gives a QR-code certificate[25].1Heath does not replace U-WIN. The family can keep the certificate in the child's record, so any doctor sees which vaccines are due.Planned

What 1Heath Cannot Fix

  • Too few people. About 13 doctors, nurses and midwives are actually working for every 10,000 Indians, far below the 44.5 that the World Health Organization once used as a reference point[28]. WHO itself says this is a comparison point, not a recommended minimum[29]. Software can save their time; it cannot create them.
  • Too few beds and specialists. Government hospitals have about 6 beds for every 10,000 people[30], and rural health centres are missing 4 in 5 of their specialists[31].
  • Too little public money. Government health spending was 1.43% of GDP in 2022–23[4].
  • Unqualified practitioners. In a survey of 1,519 villages, 68% of rural primary care providers had no formal medical training[32]. 1Heath is built for registered doctors and does not reach them.
  • Medicine quality at the factory, food, air and roads. See Indian healthcare for these.

What it can do is make each scarce minute, bed and rupee go further, by helping make sure nobody treats, tests or buys twice because a piece of paper was missing.
